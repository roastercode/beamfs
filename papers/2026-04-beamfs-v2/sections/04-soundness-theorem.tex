\section{Soundness and Saturation Theorems}\label{sec:soundness}

% Override theorem numbering for this section: v2.1, v2.2 explicit
% (uses thedefinition because aliascnt makes definition the real counter)
\renewcommand{\thedefinition}{v2.\arabic{definition}}
\setcounter{definition}{0}

We state three theorems. \cref{thm:soundness-v21} (Theorem v2.1)
covers the recovery of states perturbed by an EMR event whose
per-subblock symbol-error count is within the Reed--Solomon
correction capacity. The saturation regime is stated in two parts.
\cref{thm:saturation-v22a} (Theorem v2.2a) covers the regime on
regions whose on-disk representation carries an integrity field
(CRC32 in BEAMFS format v5: the inode region and the superblock
region); on such regions the recovery operator detects silent
miscorrection by post-decode integrity recomputation, returns
\(\bot\), and emits a journal entry flagged uncorrectable.
\cref{thm:saturation-v22b} (Theorem v2.2b) covers the regime on
regions whose on-disk representation does not carry an integrity
field independent of the Reed--Solomon codeword (data regions in
BEAMFS format v5); it is stated as a conjecture conditional on an
on-disk format v6 satisfying the integrity-field hypothesis. The
conditional form is the honest statement of what format v5
permits us to prove: \S3.11 of the reference implementation's
\texttt{Documentation/known-limitations.md}~\cite{beamfs-impl}
records the empirical falsification of the unconditional v2.2
statement on format v5 data blocks.

\subsection{Theorem v2.1: recovery under bounded EMR perturbation}

\begin{theorem}[Recovery under bounded EMR perturbation]\label{thm:soundness-v21}
Let~\(\Lambda\) be a layout satisfying invariants
\textbf{I1}--\textbf{I3} of \cref{sub:invariants}, with the INLINE
protection scheme applying \(\mathrm{RS}(n, k)\) per subblock with
\(m\) subblocks per disk block (\(m = 16\) for BEAMFS v2 INLINE). Let~\(\mathcal{G}\) be the recovery
operator of \cref{sec:m2c}, satisfying \textbf{I4}--\textbf{I5}.
Let~\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\) be the family of electromagnetic
perturbation events such that, for every \(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\)
and every state~\(s\), the number of corrupted symbols in any single
RS subblock of~\(e(s)\) does not exceed the per-subblock correction
capacity \(t = \lfloor (n-k)/2 \rfloor\) declared by~\(\Lambda\).
Then for every initial consistent state~\(s_0\) (i.e.\ \(s_0 \models\) per \cref{def:consistent}) and every
\(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\), there exists~\(K \le |\Lambda|\)
such that
\[
  \mathcal{G}^{K}\bigl(e(s_0)\bigr) \models
  \quad\text{or}\quad
  \mathcal{G}^{K}\bigl(e(s_0)\bigr) = \bot,
\]
where~\(|\Lambda|\) denotes the number of regions in~\(\Lambda\).
\end{theorem}

The full proof appears in \cref{app:proof}. The argument is the
same termination/safety/coverage structure as in the v1 retracted
theorem; what changes is the family quantified over.

\paragraph{Termination.}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:descent} (\cref{app:proof}), the function~\(N\)
counting non-consistent regions strictly decreases on each
non-fail-closed iteration. Since~\(N \in \{0, 1, \dots, |\Lambda|\}\)
and~\(N = 0\) implies consistency by \cref{def:consistent}, the
recursion terminates in at most~\(|\Lambda|\) iterations.

\paragraph{Safety.}
At every iteration, if any step of \(\mathcal{G}\) detects a CRC
mismatch after RS decode or a sentinel violation,~\(\bot\) is
returned. By \cref{prop:absorb} (\cref{app:proof}), fail-closed is
absorbing: no subsequent iteration can return a state that
silently masks the violation.

\paragraph{Coverage of the perturbation family.}
The hypothesis on~\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\) (no event corrupts
more than~\(t\) symbols in any single subblock) is exactly the RS
code's correction capacity. Events that violate this hypothesis
are addressed by \cref{thm:saturation-v22a} on the CRC-protected
regions and by \cref{thm:saturation-v22b} (conditional on format
v6) on the data regions.

\subsection{Theorem v2.2a: saturation observability on CRC-protected regions}

\begin{theorem}[Saturation observability, CRC-protected regions]\label{thm:saturation-v22a}
Let~\(\Lambda\) and~\(\mathcal{G}\) be as in
\cref{thm:soundness-v21}. Let \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}} \subseteq \Lambda\)
be the subset of regions whose on-disk representation carries an
integrity field \(c\) computed over the region payload excluding
\(c\) itself, such that the recomputation of \(c\) on the decoded
payload after RS decode is a soundness predicate (in BEAMFS format
v5, \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\) consists of the inode region and
the superblock region, each carrying a 32-bit CRC over its
payload~\cite{beamfs-impl}). Let~\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sat}}\) be
the family of electromagnetic perturbation events such that, for
some \(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sat}}\) and some state~\(s\),
there exists at least one RS subblock of a region
\(R \in \Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\) of \(e(s)\) whose symbol-error
count exceeds~\(t\). Then for every consistent~\(s_0\) and every
\(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sat}}\):
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\mathcal{G}\bigl(e(s_0)\bigr) = \bot\); and
\item the persistent journal region of~\(\Lambda\) contains, after
  the application of~\(\mathcal{G}\), an entry tagged with
  \texttt{BEAMFS\_RS\_EVENT\_FLAG\_UNCORRECTABLE} whose
  \((\text{block},\text{subblock})\) coordinates identify a
  saturating subblock of~\(e(s_0)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof appears in \cref{app:proof}. The argument has two parts.

\paragraph{Fail-closed.}
The kernel \texttt{lib/reed\_solomon} decoder
\texttt{decode\_rs8}~\cite{kernelrslib} returns the number of
corrected symbols on success (\(\mathrm{nerr} \geq 0\)) and a
negative value (\texttt{-EBADMSG}) when the received word is at
distance \(> t\) from every codeword and the decoder detects this.
A saturating event may, however, drive the decoder to converge to a
\emph{different} valid codeword than the originally encoded one
(silent miscorrection), in which case \(\mathrm{nerr} \geq 0\) is
returned and the decode return value alone cannot distinguish the
event from a legitimate correction. On a region
\(R \in \Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\), the operator's step 1 recomputes
the region's CRC32 on the decoded payload and rejects the decode
if the recomputed CRC32 does not match the on-disk value: the CRC32
acts as a soundness predicate independent of the Reed--Solomon code
algebra, so a silent miscorrection driving the decoded payload
toward a different valid codeword fails the recomputation with
probability \(1 - 2^{-32}\) per event. On rejection, the operator
proceeds to step 5 and returns~\(\bot\) by I5.

\paragraph{Observability.}
Before returning~\(\bot\), the operator emits a journal entry by
calling \texttt{beamfs\_log\_rs\_event} with positions \(=\)
\texttt{NULL} and \texttt{n\_positions} \(=\) 0, accepted by the
on-disk format v4 specification as the canonical encoding of a
saturating event. The entry includes the affected
\((\text{block},\text{subblock})\) coordinates and the
\texttt{BEAMFS\_RS\_EVENT\_FLAG\_UNCORRECTABLE} flag (bit 1 of
\texttt{re\_flags}~\cite{beamfs-impl}). The entry is committed to
persistent storage before the operator returns.

\subsection{Theorem v2.2b: saturation observability on data regions
(conjecture conditional on format v6)}

\begin{theorem}[Saturation observability, data regions, conjecture]\label{thm:saturation-v22b}
Let~\(\Lambda\) and~\(\mathcal{G}\) be as in
\cref{thm:soundness-v21}. Let \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{data}} \subseteq \Lambda\)
be the subset of regions whose on-disk representation is the data
payload of a regular file. Assume an on-disk format v6 in which
every region \(R \in \Lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\) carries an integrity
field \(c\) of width \(w \geq 32\) bits computed over \(R\)'s
codeword payload by a hash function whose preimage resistance is
independent of the Reed--Solomon code algebra (call this the
\emph{format v6 hypothesis}). Then, under the format v6 hypothesis,
the conclusions of \cref{thm:saturation-v22a} hold for the family
\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sat}}\) restricted to \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\).
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Status.}
The format v6 hypothesis is not satisfied by the present on-disk
format v5: data blocks carry their Reed--Solomon parity but no
integrity field independent of the codeword. The reference
implementation's
\texttt{Documentation/known-limitations.md}~\cite{beamfs-impl}
records under \S3.11 an empirical event of silent Reed--Solomon
miscorrection on a data block under high-density EM injection that
falsifies the unconditional v2.2 statement on format v5. The
conjecture is therefore stated, in the spirit of the falsifiability
discipline of the present report, as a contract that format v6
must satisfy and that the v6 implementation must demonstrate by an
active saturation falsification protocol on data blocks. The
mitigation path is documented in \S3.11 of the reference
implementation as candidate M2 (per-block CRC32 in format v6).

\subsection{Discussion}

\paragraph{What these theorems do, and do not, prove.}
\Cref{thm:soundness-v21,thm:saturation-v22a} establish, on
\(\Lambda \setminus \Lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\) under format v5, that
the recovery operator either returns to consistency or to
fail-closed in a bounded number of steps under the EMR-bounded
hypothesis, and that fail-closed is auditable, in the sense that
a subsequent inspection of the journal identifies the saturating
coordinates. \Cref{thm:saturation-v22b} extends the contract to
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\) conditionally on format v6, and is
stated as a conjecture to be discharged by the v6 implementation.
They do \emph{not} prove that the kernel implementation of
\(\mathcal{G}\) is bug-free: a buggy implementation can violate
\textbf{I4} or \textbf{I5} even with a correct operator
definition. \Cref{sec:m2c} provides a traceability table pairing
each operator step with the kernel function that realizes it; an
implementation audit against this table is a necessary condition
for the theorems to apply to the implementation, not a sufficient
one. A verified compilation chain (in the seL4~\cite{klein2009sel4}
or FSCQ~\cite{chen2015fscq} sense) remains the only way to close
the implementation gap; we mark this as v3+ work.

\paragraph{Why the bound is~\(|\Lambda|\).}
The bound scales with the number of regions because the worst
case is a perturbation that corrupts every region simultaneously
up to the per-subblock capacity. This worst case is unrealistic
for Family A under environmental backgrounds: \cref{sec:emr-threat}
establishes that simultaneous saturation of all regions requires
fluences orders of magnitude beyond environmental SEU rates. For
Family B, simultaneous saturation is the adversary's stated
objective, so the conservative bound is appropriate. The
per-iteration cost is dominated by a single \(\mathrm{RS}(255,239)\)
decode of \(O(n^2)\) GF operations, sub-millisecond on
contemporary CPUs.

\paragraph{Comparison with crash-consistency proofs.}
FSCQ~\cite{chen2015fscq} proves that a filesystem returns to a
consistent state after a crash. The hypothesis there is that the
storage substrate is \emph{benevolent}: it preserves whatever was
written, exactly. The present soundness theorem assumes the
substrate is \emph{adversarial} within the EMR-bounded family,
and the filesystem must recover. The two proofs compose: a
BEAMFS-compliant implementation satisfying
\cref{thm:soundness-v21,thm:saturation-v22a} \emph{and} satisfying
crash-Hoare logic in the FSCQ sense recovers from the union of
both threat models on \(\Lambda \setminus \Lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\)
under format v5, and on all of \(\Lambda\) under format v6 if and
when \cref{thm:saturation-v22b}'s conditional hypothesis is met. Mechanizing this composition is left for
future work.

\paragraph{Falsifiability.}
By construction, all three theorems are falsifiable. A
counter-example to \cref{thm:soundness-v21} is any
fault-injection trace exhibiting a state \(e(s_0)\) under
\(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\) for which
\(\mathcal{G}^K(e(s_0)) \neq \models\) and
\(\mathcal{G}^K(e(s_0)) \neq \bot\). A counter-example to
\cref{thm:saturation-v22a} is a trace under
\(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sat}}\) targeting a region of
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\) for which \(\mathcal{G}\) does not
return \(\bot\) or fails to emit the
\texttt{UNCORRECTABLE}-flagged journal entry. A falsification of
\cref{thm:saturation-v22b} requires first that a format v6
satisfying the integrity-field hypothesis exists, after which the
same trace shape on data regions would constitute the
counter-example; until format v6 lands, v2.2b is a conjecture and
\S3.11 of the reference implementation already records the
unconditional v2.2's empirical falsification on format v5 data
blocks. The empirical section (\cref{sec:empirical}) reports a
corroboration of \cref{thm:soundness-v21} by direct disk
corruption on the canary block; an active saturation falsification
protocol against \cref{thm:saturation-v22a} on the inode and
superblock regions is part of the post-Zenodo continuation.
