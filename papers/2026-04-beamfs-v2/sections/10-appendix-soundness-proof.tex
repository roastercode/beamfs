\section{Appendix: proofs of \cref{thm:soundness-v21,thm:saturation-v22a} and status of \cref{thm:saturation-v22b}}\label{app:proof}

\subsection{Auxiliary results}

\begin{lemma}[Strict descent]\label{lem:descent}
Let \(s = (s_V, s_P) \in \mathcal{S}\) and let
\(N(s) \;\stackrel{\mathrm{def}}{=}\; \#\{R \in \Lambda : s \not\models_R\}\),
the number of layout regions that fail per-region consistency.
If \(s \neq \bot\) and \(N(s) > 0\), then either
\(\mathcal{G}(s) = \bot\) or \(N(\mathcal{G}(s)) < N(s)\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By construction of \(\mathcal{G}\) (\cref{def:G}). Step 1
(decode) examines each inconsistent region. For each such region,
either every subblock decode returns \(\mathrm{nerr} \geq 0\) and
(if the region belongs to \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\)) the
post-decode CRC32 recomputation matches the on-disk value, in
which case the decoded content is installed and \(N\) decreases
by one for that region; or at least one subblock decode returns
\texttt{-EBADMSG}, or the post-decode CRC32 recomputation fails
on a \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\) region, in which case step 5
emits the journal entry and the operator returns \(\bot\) by I5.
On a region outside \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\) under format v5,
the operator has no second-source predicate available: a silent
miscorrection within the decoder's \(\mathrm{nerr} \geq 0\) arm
is, in this case, not detected. This residual case is the explicit
content of \cref{thm:saturation-v22b} stated as a conjecture
conditional on format v6. In every detected case, an iteration
that does not return \(\bot\) strictly decreases \(N\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Fail-closed absorption]\label{prop:absorb}
If \(\mathcal{G}(s) = \bot\) for some \(s\), then
\(\mathcal{G}(\bot) = \bot\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Direct from invariant I5: fail-closed monotonicity. The proposition
restates I5 in operator form.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:soundness-v21}}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:soundness-v21}]
Fix a consistent initial state \(s_0 \models\), an event
\(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\), and let \(s_1 = e(s_0)\).
Define the iteration \(s_{i+1} = \mathcal{G}(s_i)\) for
\(i \geq 1\), with the convention that if \(s_i = \bot\) then
\(s_{i+1} = \bot\) (by \cref{prop:absorb}, this is consistent
with the operator's behaviour).

By the EMR-bounded hypothesis on \(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\),
no subblock of \(s_1\) carries more than \(t\) symbol errors. The
RS decoder is well-defined within capacity, returning the unique
codeword within Hamming distance~\(t\) of the received word
(MacWilliams--Sloane~\cite{macwilliams1977}, Ch.~9); the kernel
\texttt{lib/reed\_solomon}~\cite{kernelrslib} implements this and
returns \(\mathrm{nerr} = \) (number of corrected symbols) \(\geq 0\)
on every such subblock. Hence step 1 of \cref{def:G} succeeds on
every region of \(\Lambda\) on which \(s_1\) differs from
\(s_0\). On regions of \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\), the post-decode
CRC32 recomputation matches the on-disk value because the decoded
payload is, by the within-capacity decoder property, the unique
codeword originally encoded; the saturation handling of step 5 is
not invoked.

By \cref{lem:descent}, if \(s_i \neq \bot\) and \(N(s_i) > 0\),
then \(N(s_{i+1}) < N(s_i)\) and \(s_{i+1} \neq \bot\) (because
the decoder is within capacity by hypothesis). Since
\(N(s_i) \in \{0, 1, \dots, |\Lambda|\}\), there exists a least
\(K \le |\Lambda|\) such that either \(N(s_K) = 0\) or
\(s_K = \bot\). The first case implies \(s_K \models\) by
\cref{def:consistent}; the second is the fail-closed exit.

The fail-closed exit is reached only if a sentinel verification
(step 4) failed, which is a violation of I2 not covered by the
RS-decode hypothesis. Such a violation can occur if the EMR
event corrupted a region in a way that bypasses the RS protection
(for instance, by writing canonical-looking bytes whose CRC
happens to validate, an event of probability bounded by \(2^{-c}\) for a \(c\)-bit
CRC; BEAMFS v2 uses CRC-32, so this probability is approximately
\(2.3 \cdot 10^{-10}\) per region per RS-decode event); the
operator's response is the designed safe failure. This case is bounded by I2's sentinel separation
property and does not affect the termination claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of \cref{thm:saturation-v22a}}

\begin{proof}[Proof of \cref{thm:saturation-v22a}]
Fix a consistent \(s_0\) and an event
\(e \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sat}}\) saturating a subblock of a
region \(R \in \Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\). Let \(s_1 = e(s_0)\).
Apply \(\mathcal{G}\) to \(s_1\).

Step 1 of \cref{def:G} (decode) invokes the RS decoder on every
subblock of \(R\). The kernel decoder
\texttt{decode\_rs8}~\cite{kernelrslib} on the saturating
subblock has two possible outcomes. Outcome (i): the decoder
detects the out-of-capacity condition and returns
\texttt{-EBADMSG}. Outcome (ii): the decoder converges to a valid
codeword distinct from the originally encoded one (a silent
miscorrection) and returns \(\mathrm{nerr} \geq 0\). In outcome
(i), step 1 directly transitions to step 5. In outcome (ii),
step 1 proceeds to recompute the region's CRC32 over the decoded
payload and compares it with the on-disk CRC32 value: because
\(R \in \Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\), the CRC32 is a well-defined
soundness predicate over the decoded payload, and because the
CRC32 algebra is independent of the Reed--Solomon code algebra,
the probability that the silently-miscorrected payload accidentally
satisfies the on-disk CRC32 is \(2^{-32}\) per event; with
overwhelming probability the recomputation fails and step 1
transitions to step 5.

Step 5 (saturation log) calls \texttt{beamfs\_log\_rs\_event}
with \texttt{positions} \(=\) \texttt{NULL},
\texttt{n\_positions} \(=\) 0, and
\texttt{flags |= BEAMFS\_RS\_EVENT\_FLAG\_UNCORRECTABLE}, with
the affected \((\text{block}, \text{subblock})\) coordinates
identified by either the \texttt{-EBADMSG} return or the CRC32
mismatch. The journal entry is committed to persistent storage by
the standard buffer-write sequence (\texttt{mark\_buffer\_dirty},
\texttt{sync\_dirty\_buffer}). We assume the implementation
commits the journal entry to persistent storage before returning,
an assumption realized by the kernel function
\texttt{beamfs\_log\_rs\_event} which calls
\texttt{sync\_dirty\_buffer} before its return path; this is
verifiable by audit and is the on-disk format v4
contract~\cite{beamfs-impl}.

The operator returns \(\bot\). Conclusion (1) holds modulo the
\(2^{-32}\) accidental CRC collision in outcome (ii), which is
the irreducible noise of a 32-bit integrity field and the only
gap closed by widening the integrity field in any future format
revision. The journal entry committed in step 5 satisfies
conclusion (2): it is tagged with \texttt{UNCORRECTABLE}, and
its \((\text{block}, \text{subblock})\) coordinates correspond
to a saturating subblock of \(e(s_0)\) by construction.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Status of \cref{thm:saturation-v22b}}

\Cref{thm:saturation-v22b} is, in the present report, a
conjecture conditional on an on-disk format v6 satisfying the
integrity-field hypothesis stated in its premise. The proof
structure of \cref{thm:saturation-v22a} carries over verbatim
once the format v6 hypothesis is met: replace
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\) with the union of
\(\Lambda_{\mathrm{crc}}\) and the format v6
integrity-protected data subset of \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{data}}\),
and replace the CRC32 of step 1 with the format v6 integrity
function. The substantive open work is therefore not the proof
but the on-disk format v6 specification, the \texttt{mkfs.beamfs}
update, and the kernel reader update. Until format v6 lands, the
present report acknowledges the falsification of the unconditional
v2.2 statement on format v5 data blocks documented in
\texttt{Documentation/known-limitations.md}~\cite{beamfs-impl}
\S3.11.

\subsection{Remark on falsification scope}

Both proofs assume that the kernel implementation realizes the
operator definition of \cref{def:G} faithfully. Implementation
faithfulness is not the subject of the theorem and is addressed
by the traceability table of \cref{tab:m2c-trace}, by
\texttt{checkpatch.pl} compliance per \cref{sec:m2c}, and
ultimately by mechanized verification (v3+ work). A
counter-example to either theorem produced by RadFI or by an
equivalent fault-injection methodology against the actual kernel
binary may be either a counter-example to the theorem
(\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{EMR}}\) is too narrow,
\(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{sat}}\) is mis-stated) or a counter-example
to the implementation faithfulness (a kernel bug, not a
mathematical defect). Distinguishing the two cases is the proper
diagnosis task following any falsification report.
